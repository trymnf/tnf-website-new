I love the sign you find above the sink in some airplane bathrooms: "As a courtesy to the next passenger, may we suggest you use towel to wipe off the wash basin."  


{{< figure
  src="https://normativesigns.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/04/courtesy.jpg"

  caption="Photo taken from [normativesigns.com](https://normativesigns.com/2013/04/17/as-a-courtesy-to-the-next-passenger/), a sublimely niche blog I stumbled across while looking for an appropriate photo."

The sign always made me smile. It's always struck me as archaic and odd with its stilted language, almost struggling to get to the point, the words almost spilling over the sign's edges. It's message is also just... a genuinely nice thing to do. The reminder works on me, at least.   

It was, of course, too good to last. On a plane recently, I discovered that the sign has gone the way of all things, streamlined out of existence. What I found in its place was this little symbol. 

{{< figure src="/images/socalled-progress.jpg" alt="The rational sign." caption="The rational pictogram." >}}

Objectively speaking it's clearly an improvement, delivering all of the message with zero of the reading comprehension. Perhaps it's [the triumph of the rational, European little green man over the stubborn, American big red word](https://slate.com/life/2010/03/the-big-red-word-vs-the-little-green-man.html). But it still made me sad. All the whimsy is gone, all the top hat-wearing formality. I, for one, refuse to call this progress.
